## Project Overview

As part of the **Global Connect Fellowship** at **Nanyang Technological University (NTU)**, this research project investigated a **single-stage Solid-State Transformer (SST)** architecture for high-density data-centre power conversion. The work was conducted under the supervision of **Assoc. Prof. Tang Yi** at the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering.

The project focused on developing a **modular SST topology for direct medium-voltage AC to low-voltage DC power delivery**, targeting the increasing power density requirements of next-generation data centres and AI computing infrastructure.

## Challenge

The rapid growth of **AI and GPU-based computing** is driving a significant increase in data-centre power consumption and power density. Conventional data-centre power architectures typically rely on multiple conversion stages, such as **AC–DC followed by DC–DC conversion**, which can introduce additional conversion losses, increase system footprint, and limit the achievable power density.

This creates a need for a more compact and efficient power-conversion architecture capable of handling **high power levels while maintaining scalability and modularity**.

## Proposed Approach

I investigated a **modular single-stage SST architecture** that combines medium-voltage AC conversion and isolated DC conversion within a single power-conversion stage. The proposed system was designed for **20 kV MVAC to 800 VDC** power delivery and consists of multiple SST cells distributed across the three phases.

The proposed architecture targets a **1 MW, 100 kHz SST system with 12 cells per phase**, enabling the overall power-conversion system to be scaled through modular cell configurations.

## Simulation & Technical Evaluation

I utilized and modified existing PLECS simulation models based on the topology presented in [1] to evaluate the proposed SST architecture under different operating conditions. The models were adjusted to suit the proposed system configuration and assess its performance for high-density data-centre applications.

The simulation study focused on:

* **Converter efficiency** under different loading conditions.
* **Power density** and the potential for reducing the footprint of the power-conversion system.
* **Dynamic performance** during varying load conditions.
* **Modular operation** of the SST cells and their contribution to system scalability.

## Key Outcomes

The study demonstrated the potential of a **modular single-stage SST** as a high-density power-conversion architecture for next-generation data centres. By integrating the conversion stages into a single SST cell and using a modular structure, the proposed approach provides a pathway toward **high-power, scalable, and compact MVAC–800 VDC conversion**.
